# Greenscape Pro — 5 AI Agents, Ranked

**The one thing that should govern this ranking:** Marcus is the constraint. Not lead volume, not crew capacity, not software. Every bottleneck in the materials terminates at the same person — he writes every proposal, does every site walk, answers 5–10 Slack pings a day, and pulls the permits. So the ranking question is not "which process is most broken." It is **which agents remove work from Marcus without requiring Marcus to change his behaviour first.** An agent that needs him to dictate notes differently, or review a queue every morning, is an agent that adds a habit to a man who ends the call saying "I am tired, I want my evenings back."

Ranked by return relative to build cost and behaviour-change risk.

---

### #1 — Closed-Lost Lead Reactivation Agent  ·  *built, deployed*

Turn 1,400 dead GHL leads into a systematic, personalised, human-approved re-engagement pipeline.

- Pulls each lead's context out of GHL (project, lost reason, messy notes, time elapsed).
- Drafts a short email in Marcus's voice that references one specific detail from the file.
- Validates the draft against hard guardrails — no pricing, no promises, no template artifacts.
- Routes every draft to a human who can **edit** it, then approve or reject. Nothing sends unreviewed.
- Pings Slack on draft-ready, send, and failure; suppresses bounces and current clients automatically.

**Replaces:** Brittany's occasional, unsystematic re-engagement blasts — and the reason they underperform. Marcus said it himself: "when it feels like a mass blast, they do not [respond]."

**ROI:** 1,400 leads × 2% conservative reactivation × $28K average = **~$784K in latent revenue**, at effectively zero acquisition cost. Drafting the entire backlog costs under $10 in tokens. Even at 0.5% it returns ~$196K.

**Why #1:** It is the only large opportunity that is *fully self-contained*. It needs no new habit from Marcus, no new data he does not already have, no undocumented judgment, and it cannot damage a live project if it misfires. It is also the only one whose downside is bounded — the worst realistic failure is a slightly awkward email to someone who already said no three years ago.

### #2 — Approval & Pricing Policy Agent

Answer Jenna's day-to-day approval questions from Marcus's own accumulated decisions, and capture the ones it cannot answer.

- Jenna asks in Slack: "customer wants to add a 6-ft seating wall, what do I charge?"
- Agent answers from a policy base built on the 200+ line-item pricing sheet plus every prior Marcus ruling.
- Genuinely novel cases escalate to Marcus — but his answer is captured as a **structured, labelled decision**, not a Slack message that scrolls away.
- Weekly digest surfaces the patterns, turning tacit judgment into a written rule book.

**Replaces:** 5–10 interruptions a day, and the rule book Marcus has promised to write for years and never will. Jenna in the transcript: "I literally just need a rule book. Half of these I could decide myself if I knew Marcus's framework."

**ROI:** Direct: ~1.5 hrs/day of fragmented founder attention, plus faster change-order turnaround. Indirect and larger: it is the only mechanism that produces the training corpus #3 depends on.

**Why #2:** This is the non-obvious one. The discovery call recorded this exchange and the auditor left it without a note — it reads like a minor annoyance. It is not. **The reason the proposal agent is dangerous to build today is that Marcus's pricing judgment is undocumented** ("nobody else knows how to do that"). This agent is the instrument that documents it, as a side effect of work Jenna is already doing. Sixty days of it and #3 becomes a materially safer build.

---

### #3 — Proposal Acceleration Assistant

Compress the 6–9 day quote cycle into 24–48 hours.

- Marcus dictates site-walk notes from the truck; agent transcribes and structures them into scope.
- Maps scope to the pricing spreadsheet, flags anything it is not confident about rather than guessing.
- Auto-briefs Carlos for a render when the job clears $30K.
- Produces a draft proposal Marcus **edits and sends**, never one that sends itself.

**Replaces:** Marcus hand-interpreting every set of notes and hand-building every proposal — the task he named first when asked what he would fire himself from.

**ROI:** Highest ceiling on the list. 35–40% of qualified leads are lost at this stage; ~150 projects/yr at $28K. Recovering even a third of that loss is **$400K+/yr**.

**Why #3 and not #1:** Because the founder's stated #1 is the highest-*value* agent and simultaneously the highest-*risk* one, and those are different questions. A wrong reactivation email is embarrassing. A wrong line item in a $60K proposal is a margin loss or a legal problem, and it lands in front of a live prospect. It also depends on both a behaviour change from Marcus (dictating notes consistently) and on judgment nobody has written down. Build it second, on top of #2's corpus, with heavy human review — not as a rushed P0.

### #4 — Post-Sign Project Concierge

Stop signed projects stalling in HOA, permit and deposit limbo.

- Tracks each project's HOA / permit / deposit state against a target timeline.
- Sends timed, escalating nudges to the customer; flags to Jenna only when a human is needed.
- Watches HOA board meeting dates so submissions do not miss a monthly cycle by two days.

**Replaces:** Jenna manually chasing a process "targeted at 2 weeks but routinely runs 4 to 6."

**ROI:** 8–12 projects in limbo × $28K = **$224K–$336K in delayed revenue** at any moment. The compounding cost is worse: every slip idles a crew Marcus is paying either way.

**Why here:** Operationally significant and it accelerates cash, but it moves *timing* rather than winning new revenue, and much of the delay sits with third parties an agent cannot hurry.

---

### #5 — Build-Phase Update Agent

Send the progress updates Marcus wants to send and manages on only ~30% of jobs.

- Triggers on CompanyCam photo uploads and Jobber milestones.
- Writes a short update in Marcus's voice; sends after a one-tap approval.
- Escalates to Jenna when a job goes quiet for more than three days.

**Replaces:** Silence, and the daily anxiety calls it produces.

**ROI:** Hardest to quantify honestly — it is referral-driven and referrals are not tracked. Marcus's own evidence is a customer saying "you are the only contractor who kept us informed."

**Why #5:** Cheapest build here and a real quality-of-life win, but it defends revenue already booked rather than creating any. Last on ROI, not on merit — and the first thing I would promote if referral attribution were tracked.

## Interdependencies

- **#2 unblocks #3.** The approvals agent is the data-collection layer for the proposal agent. Build #3 first and you are guessing at Marcus's pricing logic; build it after #2 and you are encoding it.
- **#1 feeds #3.** Reactivation increases proposal volume, so a successful #1 makes the quote bottleneck *worse* before #3 fixes it. That is a feature — it converts a diffuse problem into an urgent, measurable one.
- **#4 depends on #3's structured output** to know a project has entered post-sign state at all.
- Everything must write back to GHL. Jenna: "Everything has to be in GHL or it is not going to get used." This is a hard constraint, not a preference.

## Why my #1 is not the founder's #1

Marcus's stated #1 is quoting, and he is right that it is the biggest number. I ranked it #3 because *biggest number* and *best first build* are different questions. Quoting requires replicating judgment he has never written down, depends on him changing how he captures site-walk notes, and puts AI output in front of a live prospect with money attached. Reactivation needs none of that: the data already exists, the audience has already said no, and a human approves every message. It banks ~$784K of upside while #2 quietly assembles the documentation that makes his actual #1 safe to build. That is sequencing, not disagreement.

## One agent I considered and cut

**The marketing / content agent** — Marcus's stated #4. Cut because he refuted it himself on the call. Asked whether he is lead-volume or quote-volume constrained he answered "Quote. I cannot keep up with the leads I have," with Meta ROAS already healthy at 4–4.5x. More leads into a funnel that loses 35–40% of qualified prospects at the proposal stage makes the bottleneck worse and the ROAS look worse. Revisit once #3 ships.

Also cut: **lead pre-qualification** (4–6 wasted calls/week is real but is a form-field problem, not an agent) and **crew coaching** (Marcus's stated #3, ~$104K/yr — genuinely real money, but an order of magnitude below the top three, and he conceded on the call it bugs him personally more than it costs the business).

## Assumptions made

No clarifying questions were permitted, so: (1) "1,400+ closed-lost leads" are reachable by email — the materials confirm GHL holds email and SMS but not deliverability, so I assume meaningful decay and built automatic bounce suppression. (2) The 2% reactivation rate is deliberately conservative for a warm list; Marcus reports the tactic has already closed deals when worked. (3) $28K average project value applies to reactivated leads as to new ones. (4) GHL's API can read contacts and write notes — this build uses a seeded Postgres mirror of that contact shape rather than a live GHL integration, since GHL credentials were not available; the schema is deliberately GHL-shaped (`ghl_contact_id`) so the sync is a background job, not a rewrite.
